    02Architecture

    Five layers, one diagram

    1. 01 · Edge

      Meet visitors where they are

      A global edge answers repeat requests from the location nearest each visitor, so your servers only see the work that actually needs them.

      In the consoleSwitch Global edge on and watch packets turn around before they reach your load balancers.

    2. 02 · Compute

      A fleet that sizes itself

      Containers run behind regional load balancers. The autoscaler watches utilisation and adds or removes instances before anyone notices a slowdown.

      In the consoleTurn Autoscale off, then drag traffic past 30k and read the event log.

    3. 03 · Data

      Data that keeps up

      A managed relational database with read replicas, and an in-memory cache in front of it. Most reads never reach the disk; every write stays consistent on the primary.

      In the consoleRemove the Managed cache and watch p95 latency climb.

    4. 04 · Async

      Slow work, off the critical path

      Queues and workers take emails, exports and media processing out of the request, so a checkout never waits for a thumbnail to render.

      In the consoleLook for the white job packets peeling off towards Queue + workers.

    5. 05 · Observe

      See everything, page no one

      Metrics, traces and logs from every layer land on one timeline. Alerts fire on the symptoms customers feel, not on every blip.

      In the consolePress Simulate zone outage and follow the recovery in the log.

    05Migration

    Moving in, in four moves

    No big-bang weekend. Each move is reversible until the last one, and nothing switches over until it has handled real traffic.

    1. We read your current setup with you and draw it in the console, warts and all. The first diagram is always the honest one.

      • You bring: access to your current provider, a list of what wakes you at night
      • You get: a shared diagram and a plan you can reject

    2. Your services run on NIMBUS alongside the old stack. A copy of real traffic flows through both, and we compare responses before anyone relies on the new path.

      • Data replicates continuously from your current database
      • Nothing user-facing changes yet

    3. One percent, then ten, then half. Each step waits until error rates and latency look the same as before. Rolling back is one setting.

      • Every step is logged in the console timeline
      • You decide when each step happens

    4. With real traffic on NIMBUS, we trim what you over-provisioned out of caution and set up drills so the next bad day is a rehearsal.

      • Old infrastructure is retired only when you say so
      • Your first incident drill is on the calendar
